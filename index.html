The solution

A framework that speaks your language.

Four principles. Plain language. No jargon. Built on research into how expert entrepreneurs actually think.

H
Have
Start with your real assets
Who are you? What do you know? Who do you know? Your expertise, your network, your reputation, even your constraints are real starting points.
A
Absorb
Know what you can afford to lose
Instead of asking "what's my expected return?" ask: what can I afford to get wrong? For people with real lives, this changes everything.
V
Value Exchange
Build with people, not for them
The people in your life aren't just your audience. They help shape what you build. Most solopreneurs don't need to start with strangers.
E
Exploit the Unexpected
Surprises are signals
Good entrepreneurs aren't better at predicting the future. They're better at reading what's actually happening and adjusting when the plan changes.

The HAVE framework is built on Effectuation, a body of research by Professor Saras Sarasvathy at the University of Virginia's Darden School. She studied 27 expert entrepreneurs and found that they don't start with goals and work backwards. They start with what they have: who they are, what they know, who they know.

"To the extent that we can control the future, we do not need to predict it." — Saras Sarasvathy

The research has been taken up by Stanford, MIT, IMD, and INSEAD over the past two decades. This framework takes that same thinking and makes it usable for solopreneurs, without the academic overhead.

Source: Sarasvathy, S. D. (2001). Causation and Effectuation: Toward a Theoretical Shift from Economic Inevitability to Entrepreneurial Contingency. Academy of Management Review, 26(2), 243–263. Further reading: Effectuation: Elements of Entrepreneurial Expertise (Edward Elgar, 2008) and effectuation.org.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions.

The HAVE framework is an entrepreneurship methodology for solopreneurs built on four principles: Have (start with your real assets), Absorb (make survivable bets), Value Exchange (build with people not for them), and Exploit the Unexpected (treat surprises as signals). It is rooted in effectuation theory, developed by researcher Saras Sarasvathy based on how expert entrepreneurs actually think.
Lean Startup and Business Model Canvas both start with a goal or hypothesis and work toward it. The HAVE framework starts with who you are and what you have, and lets the goal develop from there. It's designed for people building without outside funding, partners, or a pre-defined market.
