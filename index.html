The HAVE framework

Four principles. Plain language. No jargon.

Rooted in effectuation — how expert entrepreneurs actually think — adapted for people building independently. Select each principle to explore.

H — Have: start with what you already own

Who are you? What do you know? Who do you know? Your domain expertise, professional network, reputation, and even your constraints are your real starting assets. The business grows out of them — not on top of them. Someone with 15 years in any field is not starting from scratch. They're starting from wealth — most of it uncounted.

A — Absorb: the art of the survivable bet

Instead of asking "what's my expected return?" ask: what can I afford to get wrong? For real people with real responsibilities — mortgages, families, careers — this reframe is liberating. You don't need to risk everything. You need experiments where the downside is survivable and the upside compounds over time.

V — Value exchange: build with, not for

The people in your life — colleagues, former clients, collaborators — aren't just your audience. They're your co-creators. When someone commits to what you're building with their time, referrals, or feedback, they shape what it becomes. This is why solopreneurs almost never need to start with strangers.

E — Exploit the unexpected: surprises are the strategy

Every plan changes. The customer you expected isn't the one who showed up. Traditional thinking treats this as noise to manage. The HAVE framework treats it as signal to follow. Expert entrepreneurs aren't better at predicting — they're better at reading surprises and acting on them.

The HAVE framework isn't invented. It's an adaptation of Effectuation — a body of research developed by Professor Saras Sarasvathy at the University of Virginia's Darden School of Business in the early 2000s. Her research asked a deceptively simple question: how do expert entrepreneurs actually make decisions, especially under uncertainty?

Sarasvathy studied 27 founders who had each launched multiple companies, taken at least one public, and operated across diverse industries. What she found contradicted conventional business school teaching almost entirely. Expert entrepreneurs don't start with goals and work backwards. They start with means — who they are, what they know, who they know — and let outcomes emerge through action and co-creation.

"To the extent that we can control the future, we do not need to predict it." — Saras Sarasvathy

Effectuation has since been validated by over two decades of academic research and adopted by entrepreneurship programs at Stanford, MIT, IMD, and INSEAD. It's the empirical counterweight to the prediction-and-plan logic that dominates most business education — and the reason it works so well for solopreneurs and independent builders, who rarely have the data, capital, or team to operate causally.

How HAVE maps to the five effectuation principles

Bird-in-Hand → Have

Start with your means: who you are, what you know, who you know. Don't begin with a goal — begin with what's already in your hands.

Affordable Loss → Absorb

Instead of calculating expected returns, define what you can afford to lose. Make bets where the downside is survivable and reversible.

Crazy Quilt → Value Exchange

Build with stakeholders who self-select in, rather than competing in a pre-defined market. Co-create with whoever shows up.

Lemonade + Pilot-in-the-Plane → Exploit the unexpected

Embrace surprises as resources. The future isn't predicted — it's built through the interactions you and your stakeholders create.

The HAVE framework simplifies and modernises effectuation for one specific audience: people building independently. It keeps the rigor of the original research while removing the academic language and adding the modern context of solopreneurship.

Source: Sarasvathy, S. D. (2001). Causation and Effectuation: Toward a Theoretical Shift from Economic Inevitability to Entrepreneurial Contingency. Academy of Management Review, 26(2), 243–263. Further reading: Effectuation: Elements of Entrepreneurial Expertise (Edward Elgar, 2008) and the Society for Effectual Action at effectuation.org.

The AI Layer

Already comfortable with the HAVE framework? This optional series explores how AI amplifies each principle — for those who are ready to add it to their toolkit. It sits outside the core curriculum deliberately: the framework works without it, and works better with it once you have the foundation.

AI + Have

AI as a Have asset

AI only amplifies in proportion to your existing expertise. Your knowledge makes AI output better than any generalist using the same tool — which means the framework still starts with you.

AI + Absorb

The new experimentation economy

AI dramatically lowers the cost of experimentation. Things that once required hiring — design, copy, research — can now be tested solo at very low cost. Your affordable loss budget goes further.

AI + Value exchange

What stays human

AI handles production. People handle meaning and commitment. Understanding that distinction makes your co-creation conversations richer without replacing the trust that builds a real business.

AI + Exploit

Pivoting at speed

When unexpected traction appears, AI lets you research, reframe, and test new directions quickly. Pivoting intelligently used to require a team. For the prepared solopreneur, it no longer does.

The HAVE framework is an entrepreneurship methodology for solopreneurs built on four principles: Have (start with your real assets), Absorb (make survivable bets), Value Exchange (build with people not for them), and Exploit the Unexpected (treat surprises as signals). It is rooted in effectuation theory, developed by researcher Saras Sarasvathy based on how expert entrepreneurs actually think.
Lean Startup and Business Model Canvas use causal reasoning — start with a goal or hypothesis and test it. The HAVE framework uses effectual reasoning — start with who you are and what you have, and let the goal emerge. It is designed for people building without funding, co-founders, or a pre-defined market.
